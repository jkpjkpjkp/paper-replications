WHAT HAS BEEN RULED OUT OR NARROWED
1. Metric aggregation and daily-return arithmetic.
   Replaying the three author-shipped 40-asset position files recovers all
   eight published mean/SD cells. The notebook's aggregation uses the same
   sample standard deviation. Fresh positions also independently reproduce
   their saved daily returns. The paper numbers are not just the result of
   using a different SD convention in our comparison.
   Evidence: reproduction/author_shipped_arithmetic_only/table1_comparison.csv;
   reproduction/fresh_local_comparison/daily_replay_audit.csv;
   1_main_result.ipynb, cells 0-7 (zero-based).

2. Wrong 40-asset architecture or changed batch size.
   The actual training log agrees with runfile/test.sh: first 40 columns,
   W=60, H=20, d_model=8, heads=4, layers=2, hidden_c=8, ff_dim=32,
   dropout=0.1, temperature=1.3, batch=64, Adam lr=0.001, zero costs.
   Tracked author source is unchanged. The 40-asset run used ordinary GPU
   memory allocation; the managed-memory workaround for 50 assets cannot
   explain the 40-asset mismatch. Its hyperparameters are inside Table 4's
   stated grid. The 50-asset hidden-dimension mismatch is a separate issue.

3. Evaluation-input cache or checkpoint reconstruction error.
   For all 63 actual allocation decisions, original Dataset_Sig input arrays
   match the cached arrays exactly. All three saved fresh checkpoints were
   replayed on CPU using those original inputs. Maximum portfolio-weight
   difference from the saved GPU outputs is 1.565e-7; maximum financial-metric
   difference is 8.61e-8. This is far too small to explain the reported gap.
   All 64 examples in the first shuffled training batch also match the
   original loader exactly. Previous checks sampled seven examples in each
   split, before and after scaling. This is not an exhaustive proof over
   every training example, but no cache discrepancy has been detected.
   Evidence: reproduction/discrepancy40/forensic_checks.json;
   reproduction/discrepancy40/first_training_batch_check.json;
   reproduction/cache_equivalence_40.json. Scripts are preserved alongside.

4. Different evaluation span between fresh and shipped SIT.
   Both SIT sets have the same 1255 return intervals. The separate EW
   baseline has 1237 intervals. On common economic end dates, fresh SIT's
   Sharpe is 0.514768 versus EW 0.575871, and wealth 1.519204 versus
   EW 1.643885. Fixing the SIT/EW date alignment does not restore the
   published Sharpe/wealth advantage in this rerun.
   Evidence: reproduction/fresh_local_common_dates/date_alignment_sensitivity_means.csv.

5. Public-code drift after the results were uploaded.
   The complete history of the pinned Git branch has been inspected. Training entry,
   launcher, model, loss/evaluation, data factory, cache builder, input CSV
   and result notebook are byte-identical to their first upload (7ca8a9e,
   2025-08-08). The loader's later edit only removes comments; its parsed
   syntax tree is identical. All ten shipped CPython 3.12 bytecode files
   also match current-source executable bytecode/constants (ignoring source
   filenames and line metadata). No older alternative training implementation
   or author training trace was found in this history. This does not recover
   any private experiments preceding the public upload.
   Evidence: reproduction/discrepancy40/repository_history.txt,
   history_source_comparison.json, shipped_bytecode_comparison.json.

CONFIRMED DIFFERENCES AND UNRESOLVED CAUSES
A. Runtime mismatch — plausible contributor, not yet attributed.
   Author requirements specify torch 2.6.0a0+ecf3bae40a.nv25.1; our local
   runs used torch 2.4.1+cu121 on RTX 4060. NVIDIA's official 25.01 notes
   map that exact author build to CUDA 12.8, distinct from our CUDA 12.1.
   The author GPU, numerical flags, checkpoints and training traces are
   not released here. Numerical/random-kernel differences remain possible.
   Our matrix TF32 flag was False. NVIDIA's 22.06 release notes document
   TF32 enabled by default in its containers, but the inspected 25.01 notes
   do not restate that flag. The TF32-on sensitivity is now complete and does
   not recover the published row. It cannot establish the author's actual
   setting or recreate their build.
   Evidence: requirements.txt; reproduction/fresh_local/environment_manifest.json;
   NVIDIA release-note URLs in References.

B. Paper versus released training protocol — definite provenance gap.
   Appendix D says five independent seeds, up to 100 epochs, patience 10,
   and a validation-selected hyperparameter grid. The released launcher
   requests three repetitions; run.py sets seed 2023 only once, uses at most
   30 epochs and patience 3. The three shipped 40-asset runs alone reproduce
   every published mean/SD cell; the two additional claimed runs and the
   search history are not present. This does not prove what the authors ran.
   It means the complete protocol-to-table provenance cannot be verified.
   Evidence: reproduction/paper.txt, Appendix D, printed p.17;
   run.py lines 8-11, 63-65 and 98 onward; runfile/test.sh, 40-asset section.

   A higher epoch cap alone cannot explain our early stop at epochs 5/4/4.
   Longer patience was tested for the first run while retaining the released
   learning-rate schedule. It selected the identical epoch-2 checkpoint after
   stopping at epoch 12. Thus patience 3 did not truncate a later superior
   validation checkpoint for this seed within that extended stopping window.
   Because the launcher seeds only once, a changed stopping time also changes
   the RNG state available to later model initializations/data shuffles.
   This is a mechanism inferred from execution order, not a demonstrated
   explanation of the author-versus-local gap. The first run also differs,
   so later-run RNG coupling alone cannot explain the entire discrepancy.

C. Potential test-based selection — concern, not established misconduct.
   Notebook cell 6 reads test_metrics.csv aggregates, sorts by test Sharpe,
   and displays the top 40-asset configuration. If multiple configurations
   were chosen this way, that would be test-set model selection, conflicting
   with Appendix D's validation-only selection claim. However, the released
   results contain only ONE 40-asset configuration (three repetitions), so
   sorting those available files cannot itself explain the gap. Earlier
   search logs are absent. Do not assert that historical test selection or
   cherry-picking occurred based solely on this notebook operation.

D. Lookahead and universe bias — separate validity question.
   No direct future-price leak has been established in the inspected code.
   The point-in-time universe construction remains unresolved. A flaw shared
   by both runs on the same released panel would not by itself explain why
   their portfolio weights differ. See LOOKAHEAD_BIAS_AUDIT.txt.

REMAINING EXPLANATIONS: PLAUSIBLE, NOT PROVEN
GPU/runtime-dependent random streams remain a concrete possibility, beyond
the TF32 flag tested above. The official PyTorch 2.4.1 Dropout.cu source
computes its CUDA grid and Philox RNG counter increment from GPU device
properties, including multiProcessorCount and maxThreadsPerMultiProcessor.
Consequently, a fixed seed is not sufficient evidence of identical dropout
streams on different GPUs. SIT uses dropout during training. The paper does
not identify its GPU in the inspected text, and the original checkpoint/RNG
states are absent. This source-based mechanism is NOT proof that it caused
the measured finance gap; no alternate-GPU controlled run was performed.
Evidence: reproduction/discrepancy40/pytorch241_dropout_source_read.txt;
model/SIT.py dropout modules; official source URL in References.

Unknown original seeds, run resets and search/stopping history remain another
possibility. The public notebook's three-run figures are reproducible from
its supplied predictions, but those predictions cannot be regenerated by
our tested runtime/configuration. Neither this finding nor the small run
count establishes fabrication, direct lookahead leakage, or that every
correct implementation must underperform. No statistical rejection is claimed.
