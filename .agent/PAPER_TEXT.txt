Abstract

This study investigates whether visual information embedded in stock charts contains economi-

er
r

cally meaningful signals for asset pricing. Using deep learning models trained on candlestick images, we construct predictive factors via convolutional neural networks (CNN) and vision transformers (ViT). The models generate monthly probabilities of future returns, transformed into
double-sorted, value-weighted portfolios, and evaluated within a high-dimensional asset pricing

pe

framework. Employing the double-selection LASSO, we assess whether these image-driven factors contribute incremental pricing power beyond 161 established risk factors. The ViT-derived
factor exhibits robust and statistically significant stochastic discount factor loadings, indicating that it captures a novel priced dimension of risk associated with visual market heuristics

ot

and trend saliency. It consistently delivers positive and economically meaningful return spreads
across firm sizes, with superior Sharpe and Sortino ratios relative to standard benchmarks. In
contrast, the CNN-based factor shows weaker performance, particularly among large-cap stocks,

tn

suggesting limited economic relevance despite statistical significance. Factor selection patterns
reveal that ViT signals are linked to multiple economic channels (low-risk, profitability, and financing), while CNN signals primarily align with quality and safety characteristics. Overall, the

rin

results demonstrate that advanced deep learning can extract interpretable, orthogonal source of
priced risk from chart images, enriching the empirical asset pricing landscape and highlighting

ep

the potential of computer vision as a tool for understanding market dynamics.
Keywords: Vision transformer, Stock charts, Asset pricing, Factor models, Deep learning

1. Introduction

Empirical finance has advanced in return prediction through machine learning. Seminal stud-

3

ies (Gu et al., 2020, 2021; Giglio et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2024) and more recent works (Salisu

4

and Tchankam, 2022; Son and Lee, 2022; Wang et al., 2024) show that machine learning captures

5

complex interactions among financial predictors in tabular data. Yet, the potential of visually

6

derived information from stock charts remains underexplored in asset pricing.

ev

2

Jiang et al. (2023) first employed convolutional neural network (CNN) to predict return

8

directions from chart images. Extending this idea, Byun et al. (2025) applied a vision Trans-

9

former (ViT), a state-of-the-art model that uses self-attention to extract price-volume dynamics

10

from candlestick charts. Both CNN and ViT models yield predictive probabilities of future price

11

changes, which can be converted into decile portfolios ranked by expected returns. The theoret-

12

ical motivation for applying computer vision to asset pricing is rooted in the cognitive role of

13

visual information in financial markets. As noted by Lo et al. (2000), technical analysis has per-

14

sisted because its visual mode of analysis is more conducive to human cognition than numerical

15

summaries. In behavioral finance, investors with limited attention rely on simplified heuristics to

16

process complex market information efficiently (Hirshleifer and Teoh, 2003). Within this frame-

17

work, by visualizing price volatility and trading intensity, stock charts serve as highly salient

18

signals that capture investor interest and drive trading behavior (Barber and Odean, 2008). By

19

training deep learning models on these images, we aim to capture these non-linear, behaviorally

20

induced price dynamics that linear factor models miss.

tn

ot

pe

er
r

7

In this context, this paper examines whether stock chart image-based predictive signals

22

represent genuinely priced risks or simply restate known factors. Factor-sorted, value-weighted

23

portfolios are constructed, and asset pricing tests assess their contribution to the stochastic

24

discount factor (SDF). To ensure robust inference, the double-selection LASSO framework of

25

Feng et al. (2020) is used, addressing omitted variable bias in high-dimensional settings (Liu

26

et al., 2020; Bang and Ryu, 2024; Bang et al., 2024; Lashkaripour, 2024; Ouyang and Long,

27

2025). The empirical design follows Bang and Ryu (2024), who evaluated ESG-related signals

28

using the same approach. This study compares image-driven factors against 161 established risk

29

factors from leading libraries (e.g., Hou et al., 2015; Fama and French, 2015; Jensen et al., 2023).

4. Conclusion

This study demonstrates that stock chart image-based deep learning models extract econom-

194

ically meaningful risk factors that complement the existing factor zoo. Specifically, ViT factors

195

exhibit significant incremental pricing power and robust size-agnostic risk-adjusted returns. In

196

contrast, CNN factors, while statistically significant within the SDF, provide limited portfolio

197

value, largely confined to micro- and small-cap segments. The ViT factor’s superior performance

198

stems from its ability to encode complex visual price-volume dynamics into predictive representa-

199

tions that align with multiple economic channels, including low-risk, profitability, and financing

200

dimensions. This multidimensional linkage differentiates ViT signals from conventional quality

201

or profitability proxies and suggests that advanced deep learning-driven visual pattern recogni-

202

tion captures a novel, priced aspect of market behavior. Furthermore, our findings support the

203

behavioral finance hypothesis that visual price patterns serve as salient proxies for investor at-

204

tention and sentiment, containing predictive information orthogonal to standard fundamentals.

205

The evidence collectively supports the notion that chart-based signals, when extracted through

206

modern computer vision architectures, enrich the empirical asset pricing landscape by offering

207

an orthogonal and interpretable source of risk premia.
