When Forecasting Gains Do Not Establish Alpha:
An Evidence Audit of Financial Machine Learning

Abstract
Financial prediction benchmarks can reward forecasting improvements without establishing investable, risk-adjusted gains. We audit a local research collection using a speciﬁed evaluation design: Chen–Zimmermann characteristics,
exclusion of stocks below the lagged NYSE size
twentieth percentile, value-weighted top-minusbottom deciles, transaction-cost scenarios, and
Fama–French ﬁve-factor plus momentum alpha
with dependence-robust uncertainty. The collection
contains a 437-paper catalogue, but its legacy evaluations do not establish compliance with this design. One completed compatible experiment identiﬁed in the audit transfers LiMT to historical US
large-cap equities with 202 retained characteristics
and three-seed score averaging. Across 2,260 trading days in 2017–2025, its annual alpha is 5.31%
before costs (95% HAC interval [−2.54, 13.16]%,
p = 0.185) and −1.27% under a 10-basis-point alltrade cost scenario ([−9.11, 6.56]%, p = 0.750).
Neither establishes positive alpha. These ﬁndings
do not prove zero proﬁtability or inferiority to a
dummy baseline. We distinguish proﬁtability, incremental performance, and evidence of economically negligible effects, and propose publication requirements that align ﬁnancial ML claims with the
statistical and economic quantities actually tested.

Introduction

A return-prediction model may improve a benchmark score
while leaving an investor with an uncertain economic beneﬁt. The relevant claim is not exhausted by a positive return
estimate: trading rules, capitalization weights, factor exposures, implementation costs, and sampling uncertainty determine what that estimate establishes. Financial ML publications that claim proﬁtable trading should therefore identify
the investment proposition being tested and report evidence
for that proposition.
We ask what the available evidence establishes when a
ﬁnancial ML model is evaluated under an explicit investment protocol. A completed LiMT transfer does not establish signiﬁcant positive alpha under the speciﬁed design. The

broader collection, however, is not a set of completed comparable experiments, and other saved designs contain positive
gross-return evidence. This setting exposes two evaluation
problems: catalogue size can overstate empirical coverage,
and a non-signiﬁcant return estimate can be misread as proof
of baseline inferiority. An evidence audit must address both
before drawing conclusions about an architecture or the ﬁeld.
Our evaluation design, supplied before this manuscript
audit, combines individual-stock Chen–Zimmermann (CZ)
characteristics, a lagged NYSE twentieth-percentile microcap
screen, value-weighted D10−D1 portfolios, gross and aftercost returns, and FF5 plus momentum regressions. These
components have precedents in empirical asset pricing, but
their exact combination is a study speciﬁcation rather than
a universal deﬁnition of ﬁnancial usefulness. In particular,
changing a model’s input family or market is a transfer experiment, not necessarily a replication of its published claim.
The contribution is threefold. First, we separate catalogue
coverage from completed return evidence, preventing missing experiments from being counted as failures. Second, we
independently reconstruct the reported uncertainty of a completed CZ202 LiMT experiment from saved return ledgers
and factor observations. Third, we give an operational reporting standard for proﬁtability and baseline comparisons.
We do not introduce a new forecasting architecture or claim
a ﬁeld-wide impossibility result.

Related Evidence and Scope

Investability and replication. Anomaly evidence depends
on the object being reproduced. [Hou et al., 2017] emphasize microcap treatment and value weighting in an extensive
replication exercise. [Chen and Zimmermann, 2022] instead
report strong reproduction of predictors with clear original
evidence and distinguish those predictors from insigniﬁcant
or modiﬁed characteristics. Their ﬁndings caution against
treating a changed portfolio speciﬁcation as a faithful test of
every original ﬁnding. We use CZ as a standardized stockcharacteristic source, not as proof that all its constituent predictors either work or fail under our design.
Trading costs also change the question. [Novy-Marx and
Velikov, 2016] study cost mitigation and show why turnover
and entry/retention rules matter. [Avramov et al., 2023] report that restrictions involving microcaps, distress, volatility
states, and trading costs attenuate ML-based performance.

LiMT combines cross-stock and temporal modeling with
liquidity-related auxiliary objectives [Yang et al., 2026].
The completed local transfer uses a changing historical US
largest-500 common-stock universe, followed by the explicit
lagged NYSE size screen and CZ-record availability requirements. It therefore covers a large-cap subset of the eligible
stock universe, not all nonmicrocap CRSP stocks.
The ofﬁcial October 2026 CZ stock-level release supplies 209 available characteristics. Seven training-constant
columns are dropped, leaving 202. Price, Size, and STreversal are absent from the public ﬁle and are not reconstructed.
Within each source month, observed values are ranked over
the available nonmicrocap common-stock population, average ties are mapped to [−1, 1], and missing values are mapped
to neutral zero. These are stock-level predictors, not returns
on factor portfolios. Each daily input uses only the preceding calendar month’s characteristics; there is no future ﬁll or
stale carry across missing source months. The source release
documents revisions and unequal end dates for some signals
[Chen and Zimmermann, 2026].
Training covers 2008–2014; validation covers 2015–2016;
test forecasts cover 2017–2025. The implementation checks
that label realizations do not cross training/validation boundaries. It uses a 21-session lookback, the full local model
conﬁguration, three seeds, and validation daily return IC for
checkpoint selection. The three prediction scores are averaged before ranking. Training labels retain daily price-return
and liquidity-related auxiliary targets, while portfolio payoffs
use CRSP total returns. Monthly characteristics repeated in a
daily forecasting architecture are a substantive input change
from the original task.
The saved protocol ﬁxes signal at session t, assumed execution at t + 1 close, and total-return payoff at t + 2. Capitalization weights use the previous month end. The execution
projection preserves frozen positions and imposes dollar neutrality. No observed stock-loan locates or actual closing ﬁlls
are supplied. The after-cost scenario charges 10 basis points
per dollar bought or sold, including establishment and ter-

minal liquidation; it omits borrow fees, ﬁnancing, market impact, and collateral yield. We therefore call it an after-tradingcost scenario, not a fully implemented net-investor return.
The experiment is retrospective. Its source, code, and input
hashes were saved before this training run, but earlier experiments had already been examined. The speciﬁcation is not
a preregistered independent conﬁrmation. Current-vintage
CRSP and CZ data do not establish historical as-published
availability.

4.3 Independent Recalculation
The manuscript audit reads existing artifacts without retraining or selecting a new model. It checks completion of all
three seeds, frozen input hashes, saved output hashes, feature count, timestamp ordering, ledger identities, cost arithmetic, date uniqueness, factor coverage, and net exposure.
It recomputes all 36 saved intercept tests using a separate
NumPy implementation of the HAC sandwich covariance and
reconstructs the Holm adjustment. The family comprises two
weights, two costs, three windows, and three intercept speciﬁcations. This adjustment does not cover all earlier research
decisions.
We additionally compute circular paired block-bootstrap
percentile intervals for the full-period value-weighted alpha,
resampling returns and factors together in blocks of 20 or 60
sessions, with 2,000 draws per case. These are newly added,
post hoc robustness diagnostics. They do not turn an already inspected sample into conﬁrmatory evidence. Sourceholdings replay was recorded by the original experiment; our
recalculation veriﬁes the saved summaries and ledger arithmetic rather than claiming a new end-to-end raw-data reconstruction.

Results

5.1 Positive Returns Do Not Establish Positive
Alpha
The full-period ledger has 2,260 daily observations from
5 January 2017 to 31 December 2025. Value-weighted
D10−D1 earns a positive annual arithmetic mean of 9.35%
gross and 2.77% under the 10 bp cost scenario. Its CAGRs
are 7.84% and 0.97%, respectively. The corresponding meanreturn tests are inconclusive (p = 0.091 and p = 0.617). It
would be incorrect to describe these realized returns as uniformly negative.
Table 2 reports the primary risk-adjusted quantities. Gross
FF5+UMD alpha is 5.31% per year, with a 95% HAC interval
spanning −2.54% to 13.16%. After the stated trading costs,
the estimate is −1.27%, with an interval spanning −9.11% to
6.56%. Neither result establishes positive alpha even before
multiple-testing adjustment. Holm-adjusted p-values are both
1.000. Figure 1 shows the uncertainty directly.
Supplementary raw-share-volume weighting also fails to
establish positive full-period alpha: estimates are 9.13%
gross (p = 0.216) and −8.81% after costs (p = 0.233). Raw
share volume depends on share units and is not interchangeable with capitalization or dollar volume. These rows are retained to disclose the evaluated variants, not to replace the
primary weighting after observing results.

convention is an analytical deﬁnition, not a newly executed
experiment in this paper. The no-trade control has no meaningful Sharpe ratio because its volatility is zero. Its zero alpha
does not make a noisy model statistically inferior.

A Publication Standard for Return Claims

We propose the following requirements for papers that claim
proﬁtable asset-return forecasting or superior trading performance. These are recommendations, not existing IJCAI requirements.
1. Declare the economic estimand. Specify the market,
eligible universe, source vintages, information lags, holding
horizon, score direction, portfolio weights, gross/net exposure, tie rule, and benchmark. Label faithful replications,
method transfers, and exploratory adaptations separately. Explain which scientiﬁc claim the test can refute.
2. Make the backtest auditable. Retain predictions and
an ordered holdings/cash/NAV ledger, corporate-action treatment, missing-payoff counts and weights, turnover, initial
trades, liquidation, and actual departures from target positions. Report the observed size-screen removals. A positive
Sharpe ratio or a prediction table alone is insufﬁcient evidence of executable trading.
3. Report uncertainty on both returns and alpha. Give
estimates, dependence-robust standard errors, signed test
statistics, conﬁdence intervals, and p-values for gross and
stated after-cost returns. Report factor exposures and explain
factor choice. Show economically meaningful units and separate annual arithmetic quantities from compounded growth.
Test an economically motivated margin when asserting that
an effect is negligible.
4. Test paired baseline improvements. Use common return dates and comparable execution assumptions. Provide a no-information control and credible simple predictive baselines. Estimate uncertainty for the paired increment
rather than inferring superiority from separate point estimates
or separate signiﬁcance decisions. Disclose differences in
model-selection budgets and missing-outcome coverage.
5. Account for selection and implementation. Freeze
checkpoints and score direction using training/validation information. Disclose all tested seeds, horizons, universes,
costs, windows, and variants, identifying the multiplicity
family and unresolved prior search. Preserve nominal and
adjusted evidence together. Include turnover-linked cost sensitivities and distinguish assumed costs from observed ﬁlls,
borrowing availability, ﬁnancing, and impact. A zero-cost result can test signal content without establishing deployable
proﬁts.
6. Publish informative null ﬁndings. Neither an access
failure nor an unﬁnished experiment is a null return result.
Conversely, a transparent negative or inconclusive ﬁnding
can illuminate precision, domain transfer, or a model failure
mode. Statistical signiﬁcance should discipline claims rather

than become a ﬁlter that suppresses null evidence or rewards
ever larger searches. Financial NLP, credit risk, fraud detection, and risk forecasting should be evaluated against their
own declared decision objectives when proﬁtable stock selection is not the claimed contribution.

8

Limitations and Conclusion

The principal limitation is breadth: this audit identiﬁes
one completed compatible model family, not a completed
protocol-compliant census. The large-cap universe, retrospective current-vintage inputs, daily use of monthly predictors, ﬁxed training interval, and idealized execution restrict
generalization. The original LiMT paper studies different
benchmarks and inputs; this transfer cannot establish that its
original results are incorrect. Our statistical recalculation is
independent of the saved inference implementation, but relies
on the same historical market observations. The targeted literature review does not estimate a ﬁeld-wide replication rate.
The defensible empirical statement is that the completed LiMT CZ202 transfer does not establish positive
FF5+momentum alpha, either gross or under the stated cost
scenario. Its uncertainty does not establish zero proﬁtability, economic negligibility, or inferiority to a dummy baseline. Future ﬁnance ML publications making return claims
should connect them to auditable portfolios, matched baseline increments, explicit implementation assumptions, and
selection-aware statistical uncertainty. Those requirements
permit strong claims when supported and informative negative evidence when they are not.

Ethical Considerations
This manuscript concerns evaluation methodology and retrospective ﬁnancial research. No strategy was deployed by
this manuscript audit. CRSP and other licensed underlying
data require their applicable access permissions; a local ﬁle’s
existence does not establish redistribution rights. The proposed artifact release should contain permitted code, aggregate results, and data-reconstruction instructions rather than
restricted source data. Availability and release permissions
remain to be ﬁnalized by the accountable authors.
