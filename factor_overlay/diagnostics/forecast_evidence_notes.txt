POST-RESULT FORECAST DIAGNOSIS

The announcement clock has detectable risk information, but the new mean-return predictions did not reliably improve forecasts. This helps explain why additional trading failed to earn its costs. It is a predictive diagnosis, not proof of a unique causal mechanism.

Primary 2015–2025: adding cycle features worsens raw next-day forecast MSE in 34/36 factor/model pairs; median relative change +0.0903%. Median within-date/leg incremental rank correlation is -0.00318; only 6/36 are positive, and none is significantly positive at 5% even before multiple-testing correction. All 36 incremental-calibration95% intervals include zero. The median incremental-calibration slope is -0.0777; ideal calibration would be 1. The negative median is descriptive, not evidence that the true relationship is reliably negative.

The extra predictions vary by a median 7.69 bp/day across stocks, while realized signed returns vary by 248 bp/day. Small signal/noise alone does not disprove alpha; here it accompanies poor out-of-sample calibration and worse forecast error. Models are making changes that lack reliable directional support.

Holding the baseline mean fixed and substituting only the cycle model's variance improves Gaussian variance scoring in all 36/36 holdout comparisons; every improvement survives Holm across 36 (largest adjusted p = 0.00383). This isolates a gain in risk forecasting. Better risk forecasts do not by themselves imply positive incremental mean return or sufficient trading profits. The portfolio risk penalty is diagonal and tracks an equal-weight book; it does not fully optimize correlated portfolio risk.

Using the primary one-day forecasts against exact five-session average payoffs, all 36 pairs worsen MSE (median +0.516%); median incremental rank correlation is only +0.00277 and joint calibration remains near zero. Separately trained horizon-matched models also worsen MSE in all 36 pairs (median+0.0799%, equal-year mean of existing results_h5 scores). Thus horizon mismatch contributes to forecast error but is insufficient to explain or fix the underperformance.

A limited optimism check uses 2004 observations already included in the 2005 model training window: all 36 pairs improve one-day MSE there (median-0.512%), versus 34/36 worsening in 2015–2025. This is consistent with fitting unstable sample relationships; it is not a formal attribution of overfitting or proof that the clock contains no information.

Inference averages stocks within date/leg and then legs within date, with HAC12 across weekly sampled dates. IC requires at least 5 sampled stocks in a leg; sparse RDIPO short legs may be omitted from IC while remaining in other moments. The 36 model pairs are correlated; counts are descriptive rather than 36 independent replications. These diagnostic tests were designed after seeing strategy results and do not establish new trading alpha.
