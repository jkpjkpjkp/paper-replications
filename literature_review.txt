TARGETED NARRATIVE LITERATURE EXTENSION -- 10 NOVEMBER 2026

Question
What existing evidence motivates cost-aware, risk-adjusted, statistically credible
evaluation of ML return claims, and what evidence contradicts a universal assertion
that ML methods cannot profit?

Selection and search boundary
This is a single-agent targeted narrative extension, not a systematic review.
The 437-paper workbook is user-supplied and has no demonstrated population-level
coverage. Literature discovery used Google through the user's available Chrome
automation; claim assessment used author, publisher, institutional, official data,
arXiv and research-society sources. No reference search uploaded local restricted
financial data. No independent second-reviewer eligibility decisions were made.
Search result snippets were used for discovery, not as verified paper results.
No inference treats a citation, paper, market, seed or specification as an
independent replicate unless that unit actually supplies independent observations.

THEMATIC SYNTHESIS

Replication depends on matching the original question.
Hou, Xue and Zhang's Replicating Anomalies emphasizes how microcap treatment and
value weighting change inference. The available NBER page is the 2017 working
paper (447 variables, 64%), while the published 2020 version is associated with
a different count (452, 65%). The manuscript cites the opened 2017 version and
does not silently mix its counts with those of the published version. Author-site
PDF retrieval failed with HTTP400 and the publisher abstract returned HTTP403.
The NBER page's automatically supplied published-version citation unusually adds
an editor to the author list; the manuscript retains the three authors displayed
on the actual working-paper record. The broader point is supported without
quoting an unverified published-version numerical estimate.

Chen and Zimmermann (2022) provide an essential counterweight. Their published
abstract reports high reproduction among predictors with clear original evidence;
their working-paper text explains why a permissive anomaly definition and altered
weighting can yield different headline failure rates. Therefore a protocol transfer
must not automatically be called a faithful refutation of an original paper.
The 2021 working paper differs slightly from the 2022 published abstract in some
regression summary values; those values are not imported into this manuscript.
The October2026 official data page also documents revisions and early termination
of option-related signals. Current-vintage inputs are not historical as-published
archives, even when the signal code includes reporting lags.

Costs and restrictions motivate, but do not predetermine, the result.
Novy-Marx and Velikov (2016) examine turnover, cost mitigation, and net anomaly
performance. Their earlier NBER full text is locally retrieved; the author's
publication list and Crossref record verify the final publication identity.
Avramov, Cheng and Metzker (2023), via its institutional publication record and
abstract, finds that size/distress/market-state restrictions and costs attenuate
ML performance. Its abstract also retains positive findings in some settings;
this is not a claim that every strategy necessarily loses.

Positive ML evidence must be treated fairly.
Gu, Kelly and Xiu (2020) provide published full text via an author's university
site. Their design includes value-weighted deciles, FF5+momentum analysis, and a
NYSE twentieth-percentile exclusion sensitivity (published p2267). A claim that
finance ML uniformly ignores such evaluation would be false. The source reports
positive results; those are external literature findings, not independently
reproduced results in the current local protocol.

An archived American Finance Association working-paper copy by Azevedo, Hoegner
and Velikov reports profitable after-cost ML strategies and considers post-
publication decay and modern liquidity. Its abstract reports positive six-factor
alpha with a t-statistic. The manuscript uses this only as contrary evidence,
without transplanting its numerical result into our benchmark table. The author's
current site lists a 2026 version, but the accessed AFA copy is undated and its
PDF metadata indicate an older archive; the manuscript cites the archive as n.d.
and does not label its contents as the verified latest2026 version. SSRN returned
HTTP403. Current-version full-text verification remains a useful author follow-up.

Inference must cover search, dependence and the benchmark contrast.
Harvey, Liu and Zhu (2016), from the author's full published paper, motivates
multiple-testing-aware factor evidence. White (2000), from the original society
abstract and retrieved published PDF, addresses benchmark superiority after
specification search. Newey and West (1987) motivates dependence-robust covariance.
The manuscript describes the actual covariance calculation and finite-sample
reference distribution; the independent audit reconstructs all36 saved tests.
HAC assumptions and an isolated Holm family do not solve historical test reuse.

Additional candidates discovered but not used as verified substantive evidence
Hansen (2005), A Test for Superior Predictive Ability: institutional retrieval
failed; no claim is based on the inaccessible full report.
Holm (1979): title discovered, Crossref DOI lookup failed (404). The manuscript
names the procedure actually implemented and describes its family, without adding
an unverified bibliographic record or asserting DOI verification.
Recent working papers on anachronistic testing-date bias and trading costs were
discovered but not full-text assessed. Their snippets do not strengthen our result.

RESULTING POSITION
The literature supports a demand for clear economic estimands, credible costs,
paired baseline comparisons, uncertainty and selection accounting. It does not
support a universal negative statement about ML profitability. The original
contribution available from this local record is a bounded evidence audit and
one completed protocol-compatible transfer, not an exhaustive meta-analysis.
