Verified mathematical limitation — assessed 2026-11-17

The camera-ready paper says on PDF page 4 that soft neural inference preserves
hard symbolic ranking (to five decimal places), and uses this to motivate
lossless decompilation. This cannot hold universally for the finite-temperature
computation in Eqs. 1, 3–6 with Appendix Algorithm 1.

Counterexample: use two stumps on different features, each with threshold 0,
left leaf 0 and right leaf 1. Use the paper's tau1=100 and tau2=10. For one
stump, the soft right-leaf probability is
  sigmoid(2 * tau2 * tanh(tau1 * x)).
For inputs A=(1e-7, 1e-7) and B=(1, -1e-7), hard ensemble outputs are A=2 and
B=1. Soft outputs are A=1.0001000000 and B=1.4999499979. The ranking reverses.
An independent closed-form calculation and the local Algorithm 1 implementation
agree within 2e-7. Neither input lies exactly on a threshold. This is a concrete
counterexample to a universal ranking guarantee; it does not establish why the
paper's measured benchmark AUCs differ from the local reconstruction.

The earlier sign/depth specification conflict also remains: Equation 2 on PDF
page 4 assigns positive left / negative right signs and leaf-depth weights;
Appendix Algorithm 1 on page 12 assigns negative left / positive right signs
and ancestor-order weights. The local implementation follows Algorithm 1.
Both pages were visually checked against the original PDF on 2026-11-17.
